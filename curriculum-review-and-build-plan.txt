1. what-is-torque.html
Title: What is Torque? The Secret Superpower of Slow Gears
Socratic hook: "Why can the same motor spin fast but fail to lift a Bean Bag unless we slow it down?"
Key concepts:
- Torque means turning force.
- Long arms need more torque because the load is farther from the pivot.
- Gear reduction trades speed for torque.
- Mechanical advantage helps, but friction and flex steal some of the gain.
- In Flop/Level Up, torque matters when the arm lifts a Bean Bag or a mechanism resists gravity.
Images to find:
- VEX official: Flop Hero Bot arm/intake image, Level Up field/Bean Bag image.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "child opening jar", "wrench bolt close up", "bicycle gears close up", "robotics gears plastic".
- Optional CSS diagram: small gear driving big gear, with arrows labeled "fast but weak" and "slow but strong."
Try It:
- Interactive slider: "Arm length" and "Bag weight." Show "torque needed" as a growing bar.
- Question cards: "Which gear pair should lift more: 12T to 36T or 36T to 12T?"
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- Explain that Flop's arm must lift a Bean Bag reliably, not just once. Slow gearing can make a lift stronger and easier to control, but too slow can waste match time.

2. center-of-gravity.html
Title: Why Does My Robot Tip Over? Center of Gravity Explained
Socratic hook: "Why does a robot feel brave when its arm is down, then suddenly wobble when the arm rises?"
Key concepts:
- Center of gravity is the robot's balance point.
- Support polygon is the floor shape made by the wheels/contact points.
- A robot tips when its balance point moves outside its support area.
- Tall arms, quick turns, sudden stops, and high batteries can make tipping worse.
- Wider bases, lower mass, bracing, and smoother driving can help.
Images to find:
- VEX official: Level Up robot/Flop side view; VEX IQ parts/tower build image if available.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "stacking blocks tower", "leaning tower toy", "skateboard balance", "kid building tower".
- Optional CSS diagram: rectangle wheelbase with a dot moving inside/outside the support polygon.
Try It:
- Clickable "Move the Bean Bag" demo: when the bag is high/front, the balance dot moves toward the tipping edge.
- Physical try-it box: build a tower, test tipping angle, lower one heavy part, retest.
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- Flop must drive, turn, collect, and score while the arm changes the robot's balance. A safe route may beat a tall risky score if the robot tips or needs slow turns.

3. friction.html
Title: The Invisible Thief Called Friction
Socratic hook: "Where did the motor's power go if the wheel barely spins but nothing looks broken?"
Key concepts:
- Friction is a rubbing force that resists motion.
- Some friction is useful: wheels need grip, intakes need grip, claws need grip.
- Bad friction wastes motor effort: crooked shafts, squeezed spacers, rubbing gears, bent frames, and tight collars.
- A free-spin test reveals hidden friction.
- Reducing unwanted friction can make the same motor feel stronger.
Images to find:
- VEX official: VEX IQ shafts, collars, spacers, gears, wheels, chain/sprocket reference images.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "car tire on road close up", "ice skating", "sandpaper close up", "bicycle chain close up".
- Optional paired images: "grippy tire" vs "smooth ice."
Try It:
- Two-card comparison: "Good friction" and "Bad friction." Student sorts examples by tapping/clicking.
- Physical try-it box: spin a shaft with bad spacing, then add correct spacers/collars and count coast time.
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- If Flop's arm or intake binds, it may miss scores even with correct code. If wheels have the wrong friction, it may drift, scrub, or fail to align.

4. levers.html
Title: Levers: How a Small Force Lifts a Big Load
Socratic hook: "Where should you push on a beam if you want one finger to lift something heavy?"
Key concepts:
- Lever parts: effort, load, fulcrum.
- Three lever classes, explained with everyday examples.
- Moving the fulcrum changes force and distance.
- Robot arms often trade force, travel, and control.
- Levers connect directly to torque and mechanical advantage.
Images to find:
- VEX official: VEX IQ beam/pivot/arm images, Clawbot arm or Flop arm if available.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "seesaw playground", "wheelbarrow", "fishing rod", "scissors close up".
- Optional CSS diagram: three lever classes with colored dots.
Try It:
- Drag-and-drop fulcrum demo: move the pivot and watch "easy lift" vs "far movement" change.
- Physical try-it box: use a VEX beam and pin as a lever; test fulcrum close to load vs far from load.
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- When Flop lifts a Bean Bag, the arm is a turning beam. The farther the bag is from the pivot, the more torque the motor needs.

5. omni-wheels.html
Title: Omni Wheels: The Wheels That Slide Sideways
Socratic hook: "How can a wheel roll forward but also slide sideways without fighting the robot?"
Key concepts:
- Traction wheels grip strongly in many sideways directions.
- Omni wheels have little rollers that reduce sideways resistance.
- Tank/skid drive is simple and strong, but turning can scrub.
- H-drive and X-drive use wheel directions to create sideways movement.
- Holonomic drive means the robot can move in more directions without first turning.
- Tradeoffs: motor use, traction, complexity, driver practice, and Level Up shortcut needs.
Images to find:
- VEX official: VEX IQ traction wheel and omni wheel product images; Level Up field/shortcut image.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "shopping cart wheel close up", "robot wheel close up", "mecanum wheel", "forklift wheel".
- Optional CSS animation: arrows from four angled wheels combining into sideways motion.
Try It:
- Interactive wheel-arrow selector: choose tank, H-drive, X-drive; show possible movement arrows.
- Physical try-it box: push a traction wheel sideways, then push an omni wheel sideways and compare.
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- Strafing can make alignment easier near goals, but using extra motors for drive may leave fewer motors for arm/intake mechanisms under the VIQRC motor limit.

6. linkages.html
Title: Linkages: How Bars Stay Level While Moving Up
Socratic hook: "Why does one robot hand dump its load as it rises, while another hand stays almost level?"
Key concepts:
- A linkage is connected bars that constrain motion.
- A 4-bar parallelogram can keep an end piece level.
- 6-bars and chain-bars can reach higher or control orientation differently.
- More links mean more reach, but also more friction, flex, torque demand, and tipping risk.
- Rubber bands can assist lifts but can also make force change through the motion.
Images to find:
- VEX official: Clawbot arm, Flop arm/intake, VEX IQ beams/pins.
- Unsplash/Pexels: "scissor lift", "desk lamp arm", "mechanical linkage", "robot arm toy".
- Optional CSS/SVG-style diagram: four bars with opposite sides highlighted as parallel.
Try It:
- Simple animation with a vertical slider: move a 4-bar up/down and keep the "tray" level.
- Physical try-it box: build a small 4-bar with VEX beams, attach a paper cup/tray, photograph three heights.
Connection to Flop/Level Up:
- A stable scoring mechanism must lift a Bean Bag without dumping it, twisting, or tipping the robot. Linkages can help with angle control, but only if built square and low-friction.
